# Suggested Instagram Posts

These versions keep the strongest first-person turns, strip out most of the hard launch language, and stay shaped for Instagram-length captions.

## Joint Queen

The process of making this record started quietly. Walls and I had been playing together for almost two years, bringing our own songs to the table, doing good work, having a real time. But we were not at the heart of it yet.

Then John played me the tracks he had been building. The sound hit me the same way Public Enemy hit me when I was nineteen: groove, raw emotion, creative synthesis. Everything changed. As soon as I recorded a note of guitar, I was in a journey that had no analogue in my life, working with a deeply gifted musician and engineer who was also becoming my best friend and, in ways I am still learning to describe, saving my life.

Joint Queen felt like the right place to begin: joy, groove, open air, a song big enough to let the spirit in. I believe we lose joy if we do not cultivate it. This track is the sound of finding it again.

Terry Devine

## Stash Daddy

Stash Daddy started at the kitchen table with a joke, a hand-drawn logo, and a title that somehow already knew what kind of freedom the song was asking for.

At that point I still thought recording had to be sacred, expensive, tense, and over-rehearsed. Then John would set the track looping in my headphones, leave the room, and tell me to record some guitars. No lecture. No pressure. No engineer energy. Just: go create.

The first time it happened my mind locked up. What do I play? I did not rehearse anything. Then it clicked. There was no rehearsing. There was only creating, in real time, with no filter. Standing there alone with Stash Daddy thumping in the headphones, I started to understand that my creative force could actually speak as it was happening. John wrote the line, "I feel too much. It's not enough." But to say that line represents me is understated.

Stash Daddy is the sound of my heart being set free.

Terry Devine

## Space Cruiser

I wrote the first sketch of Space Cruiser the morning after one of the worst shows of my life. I woke up with that specific kind of doubt that makes you question whether you even like trying to do music at all. The night before, a friend had put on a documentary about Icelandic music, and what stayed with me was the simplest possible idea: make the music you want to hear.

So I did. I wrote the song to clear the taste out of my mouth. Then I forgot about it for months, until Joint Queen and Stash Daddy started asking for a third door. That was when it became part of the Bong Tour trilogy and part of the larger language of Volume 1.

Space Cruiser was also where the collage method really came into focus for us: delayed takes, stitched ideas, atmosphere as arrangement. Ishan added an incredible sargam part. Terry added a perfectly timed bong rip while the mic was live. Some people sample drums. Some people sample grief. Some people, with reckless abandon, sample the atmosphere.

Drive safe.

John Walls

## Home

There was a period in Los Angeles when I was moving as fast as possible in the wrong direction. By the time I came back to Salt Lake, I was craving something simpler, even if I did not yet know how to live it.

I returned to my house and found it basically empty. I slept on the floor that first night, and my nervous system reset in a way I still do not fully understand. That was the first real lesson of Home: the place itself did not care what I had done, what I had wrecked, or what story I was dragging behind me. It just received me.

Years later, I pulled an old one-take guitar recording back out while teaching Terry some Ableton techniques, and the song started gathering its final body. Family found its way into the track. So did practice, recovery, and the quieter kind of faith that arrives after survival.

I hope this song helps somebody feel their way back to themselves.

John Walls

## Decay

At some point while I was getting ready to record more guitar, John made a small remark, almost like he was tossing a match over his shoulder: "This is gonna be our metal song."

Something opened in me. I finally understood that he was not asking me to give him the version of myself I thought he wanted. He trusted me to hear what I heard and to follow it all the way through. I had not really understood before that my own intuition could be valid. Beautiful, even.

That matters when you are holding a screaming guitar in your hands. Decay is full of that kind of humanity for me. Not performance. Not pose. Soul. When I heard John's spoken words, "Death was contained in me / Expressed as life," I knew them immediately. I had never said them, but they were in me too.

These guitars are the sound of my death being expressed as life.

Terry Devine

## Resolve

There are eras that define us because they force a simple choice: rise above, or drown.

Resolve came from that territory for me. The place where you stop negotiating with yourself, stop romanticizing collapse, and start making contracts with your own heart that you are finally willing to keep. Practice becomes instinct. Intention becomes action. The self that needed to burn is not erased, but refined.

Some versions of us are meant to become ash so the truer thing can rise.

Whichever version of you is reading this, I am glad you found Resolve.

JW

## Poetry

Poetry started during a breakup and immediately gave me a ridiculous problem: how do you write a song called Poetry without writing a bad poem, a corny poem, or something that feels like a performance of having read poems?

I still do not think I have a clean answer. I stole the title from Marianne Moore because her line about "a place for the genuine" felt truer than any cleverness I could have manufactured. That became the challenge of the song: find the genuine thing underneath the performance, underneath heartbreak, underneath the vanity and fantasy and fear.

Then Terry came into the room and made the song bigger than my own experience. Some of the lines people carry out of this one are his. That feels right. Poetry stopped being my private mess and became something shared, human, and alive.

I hope it finds you when your life feels like ink spilling out.

John Walls

## Gratitude

Gratitude already meant something to me before it became the closer. But when John showed me the prospective sequence and this song was holding the final position, my relationship to it changed completely.

Something deepened in my voice. In my guitar. In my understanding of what this record had become. There was a flow-state recognition in it for me, like I was hearing the sound of a life I had always wanted but did not know how to reach. Not fantasy. Not projection. Something real.

I have wept listening to this song. I have also felt gratitude becoming an anchor in my actual life, day by day. Being part of this record changed me. It gave me a vitality I associate with peace.

With love,
Terry Devine
