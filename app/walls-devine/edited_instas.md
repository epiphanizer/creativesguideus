Joint Queen

The process of making this record started quietly. Walls and I had been playing music together for almost two years. We brought our own songs to the project. It was a pleasure to support John’s work and to get to sing my songs too. However. A dominant memory that has surfaced is that we weren’t at The Heart of It. 

Not because our songs weren’t good. Rather because the music we were playing wasn’t from Us. I played my tunes enough while fronting my own band that I wasn’t as nearly excited about them as I was about making something different.  New.

John shared recordings he’d been working on. Please understand: I’m still the same twelve year-old who got a record, put it on and started playing guitar with it immediately. These tracks? The sound…the style. It was the exact same moment as when I heard Public Enemy for the first time at nineteen: groove…raw emotion…creative synthesis. 

Everything changed. As soon as I recorded a note of guitar I was upon a journey that had no analogue in my life. Working with a deeply gifted musician and recording engineer who was also becoming my best friend. And saving my life.

I had yet to experience pure creativity like this.

In the landscape of my mind, Joint Queen - inspired by the joy that Peace Feather brought to John’s life - was exactly where we SHOULD start… joy… grooves…an open sonic landscape.

Music is joy. I believe we lose joy if we do not cultivate it. I was not lost completely, but things were not looking good before I met John Walls.

And now…here I am IN IT.  Doing the thing, not talking about it.

This tune…the groove, the energy… is a perfect introduction to the joy in our hearts.

Terry Devine

Stash Daddy (Terry, Peace Feather)

We were all sitting at the kitchen table.  I dunno how the conversation came around to the concept of Stash Daddy.  But I grabbed a piece of paper and wrote a little creative logo of the words Stash and Daddy and John stuck it to the mirror on the wall next to the table.  And we all had a good laugh.  I don’t honestly know if John had titled this track before that, but knowing the title when I recorded the first guitar parts contributed to my carefree approach to “recording”.  

You see, I didn’t understand yet what was happening.  The only experiences I ever had with recording music were ritualized, sacrosanct…expensive processes.  Lock the songs, rehearse like crazy before going in the studio.  Have a terrible time with an engineer who acted like he didn’t want to be there.

Imagine my surprise to be relaxing after a joint, John setting up the extant tracks to loop with my guitar recording…and he leaves the room.  No thoughts or advices…just…record some guitars.

The first time this happened, my mind was clenched from previous recording experiences.  What Do I Play?  I didn’t REHEARSE anything.  Gaah. !

There WAS no rehearsing.  There was only creating.  Purely.  Standing there by myself with Stash Daddy thumping in the headphones, I played.  At first I was playing along.  The parts were fine.  I think it took two more iterations of this process of JW leaving me alone to play whatever I wanted before I understood that my creative force could actually happen in real time.  And that I had to SAY SOMETHING.  With my guitar.  With my voice.  With my heart.   No filter.

“I feel too much.  Its not enough.”

John Walls wrote and delivered that line.  But to say it represents me is…understated.  Stash Daddy is the sound of my heart being set free.


Terry Devine

Space Cruiser (Sean, Ishan)

I had just played one of the worst shows of my life. The kind that makes you wonder if you even like the process of attempting to make and “do” music at all. It wasn’t that long ago - September of 2025. I was 36 and had been playing music live for 22 years.

Depressed about the whole shebang, I went to a friend's house for solace and they put on “Screaming Masterpiece”, a documentary about Icelandic music. It was amazing, and timely, to see some of the sonic influences I had idolized growing up explaining their worldviews and take on, and approach to music.

One of the main aspects I appreciated from that film and the people featured within it, was that they basically decided: “There’s no point to trying to do music here to be famous, whatever. It’s an island of 400,000 people. So we just make the music we want to hear.” (Total paraphrase).

I woke up the morning after the show and started writing an unnamed song, simply to get the taste out of my mouth from the show and to make something that I would enjoy as a listener. It was really the first song I wrote for the record, not even knowing that was what I was doing. I actually forgot about it for about five months, until the creation of Joint Queen and Stash Daddy were begging for an ending to form a sort of “Trilogy” of songs that are thematically based upon a screenplay I’d written called “Bong Tour.” 

Our friend @dustinstonebrook heard the initial mix and simply responded: “If you’re calling it Space Cruiser… it needs to be spacier.”

That was the start of forming the sort of musical template for Volume 1, which involved “collage” editing together lots of takes and ideas with delays to form things we could have never thought of had we sat to write a song the “traditional” way.

After smoking weed with Dustin, Terry and I linked up with @ishanthakur, an amazing singer and musician who I know from my travels through India. Ishan added a mesmerizing sargam part and really made the song have more of a transcendent and world music feel than any of the other tracks on the record. He says we’ll be well-received by the ganjedis in Rishikesh, whatever that means.

One night, while the mic was rolling, Terry added a bong rip to the track, and that’s when I knew the song was complete. I don’t make up the rules, I just smoke weed.

I’ve since re-discovered that “Smoke Two Joints” by Sublime also features this key sonic element (legend has it, from possibly the same bong). At the risk of being labeled derivative, I simply say that while producing harmonium-driven music in today’s rapidly evolving industry, it’s always an apt time to slow down for a historically significant bong rip in the recording studio.

Some people sample drums. Some people sample grief. Some people, with reckless abandon, sample the atmosphere.

We hope you enjoy the ride. Drive safe, my friends.


John Walls

Home (Sean)

Having gone through every drug as fast as humanly possible in a race to destroy my life in Los Angeles, circa 2021-2023, I found myself yearning for the simpler grounds and estates of Salt Lake City. I really didn’t want to leave Los Angeles, as I quite enjoyed the drugs, however, a simpler life beckoned: one I imagined might contain a family, dogs, a nine to five, and hell, even a fence. I might even sand it and stain it myself. “For pride!” I thought, before doing so.

I’ve never lived a simple life. I don’t traditionally take pride in building fences. I chose certain, horribly toxic paths simply for “kicks” or artistic inspiration. I’ve done some crazy, and (depending on who is doing the forgiving) unforgivable things in my time. I think we all have. You know who doesn’t give a shit about any of that? Home. 

I came back from Los Angeles to my house in Salt Lake about three years ago, today, at the time of this writing. I’ve always appreciated roughing it, and this return turned out no different. There was no furniture, as renters had moved their material items away. There was only really a table that my mother had given the house years earlier, and a few other small pieces of furniture. I slept on the floor that night and, fascinatingly, my nervous system reset in a way I did not imagine possible. Home. I was finally, finally, home. 

I was paid a visit by my former bandmate @swingomusic, who had been exploring a few of his own songs. Having no drugs in my system, I managed to remember to hit record. I captured his guitar without a click, and it was a one-take which remains the case in the final recording. It’s actually a song he had written for himself called “Entropy Swing” if I recall correctly. I was showing him some techniques within Abelton using his guitar track with filters as a de-facto sitar / synth layer.  I laid down a one-take vocal as well over it, and then put it away for two years. I opened it, in the proper spirit, as a lesson in Abelton with Terry in December 2024. It was my production lesson-giving song for a while and it just started gathering up beautiful parts. Finally, I connected with my sister @freyachurros to add the finishing touches to what became a rather spiritual song by the end of it, at least to me. 

Over the past few years, I’ve spent a lot of time immersing in a yoga practice to substitute for my former vices. I’ve learned to take a lot of those inward lessons with me, and my hope in sharing this song, that I sing to myself now, is that you find your home in life, and not only that, but that you may also find a way to learn how to take it with you.

Your Homie,

John Walls

Decay (Terry)

There is a theme that emerged during these recordings for me.  Maybe I had recorded some guitar for this song, but JW made a subtle remark as I was getting ready to record more.  I don’t remember the exact words, but he says something like This Is Gonna Be Our Metal Song.  Or similar.  With a chuckle and a smile.

It clicked.  It wasn’t that this crazy-talented musician/engineer had been asking me to play what I thought HE might like or want on the song that he wrote.  He trusted ME to do that according to my own creative intuition.  I literally had not understood that what I hear is…valid.  Beautiful, even.

Its a metal song, he says.

I’m different now.  Because I am listening to me.  I had just as much fun playing funky, clean guitar parts on this record.  But when I hold a screaming guitar in my hands, I am Home.  

All of these songs prompt a deeply emotional response for me.  But these guitars in particular…the wild, screaming musical humanity that came from some hours of wailing that JW organized and engineered and mixed into a coherent statement that I did not hear…they are the sound of my soul.

As I recall, John had recorded some spoken-word vocals and invited me to do the same.  When I heard his words:

Death was contained in me
Expressed as life

I knew for the first time in my entire life that there is magic.  Real magic.  I knew those words.  I never said them.  But they were in my soul too.

There is more I will share about my Era of Decay.  Suffice here to say that this record…these guitars…are the sound of my death, expressed as life.

Here…for…The Next Evolution.

Terry Devine

Resolve (Sean)

There are eras of our lives that define us. Where we are thrust into our becoming with a simple choice: Rise above, or drown. There are a thousand reasons why you could fail, important then, to focus on the reason you will succeed. Until you are thrown into the heat of it, the crucible. Failure is not an option. It is really life or death. So you practice until instinct becomes Resolve.

There are few things more powerful than when the mind comes into congruence with the body. When the soul meets the spirit and action is taken with intention and integrity. I do not believe this happens without us first finding and meeting ourselves and making our own contracts binding, with our own hearts and actions. 

And once that meeting and agreement finally happens, it’s hard to ever go back to the life you held onto before. The one that held you back. The life that feasted on your body and soul by virtue of you not knowing where you really stood: in and with yourself. 

Some selves are meant to burn. Not to be erased, but to be refined and released. They become ash so the truer thing can rise.

Whichever version of you, wherever you are. I’m glad you found Resolve. 

JW

Poetry (Sean)


“Poetry” was one of the first songs that came to the drawing board for Volume 1. I wrote the first chords and melody during a breakup, then immediately had the terrible problem of having a song called “Poetry” and not wanting to write a shitty poem. Or a corny one. Or something that felt like I was trying to prove to you, Dear Reader, that I knew what poetry was.

I confess: even after writing the song, I don’t. I only have an idea of it. Iamb what Iamb. A literal bandit. I stole the name from Marianne Moore’s poem, Poetry:

“I too, dislike it… but, there is in it after all, a place for the genuine.”

That was the challenge: to find the genuine thing underneath the performance of it. Not just the performance of music, but life. The song started with heartbreak, but didn’t stay there. Somewhere in the writing that preceded the recording, the pain changed shape. I wrote until my arm hurt. The body was in pain, but the soul was breaking free. Duality.

When @terrydevinerocks came into the studio, he made the song bigger than my own experience or conception of the song had ever been.

Some of the central lines of the song are his:

“I’ve been so many charlatans, when will I breathe?”
“Every note is personal.”
“You are poetry. You are fiercity. Write your life. Share your humanity!”

The vanity, fragility, spirituality, fantasy, bullshit, beauty. All of it.

“Most folk had choked out the fires of their fiercer experience with rubble of sentimentality and stupid fear, and rarely could he feel the hot destruction of Life fighting out its way.”
— D.H. Lawrence, A Modern Lover

As I write to you, Dear Reader, I am not only thinking of wholesale-copping more of D.H Lawrence’s lines (belonging firmly to the public domain), but also feeling the above particular statement is close to the heart of what Poetry ended up being all about. 

Not cleverness, not meter, hell, not even a clean conceit. Everything enjambed and scrambled, beyond a coherent analysis, likely to be unperformed anyhow. Yet, it’s full of life. And life is best lived with a refusal to choke the fire out of our best heartbreaks and adventures too early. It’s important to suffer the love properly, and let life fight its way through a song.


And I hope this song hits you when you are messy - when your life feels like ink spilling out. Those are the moments that make you human, and they’re worth memorializing, sharing.


John Walls
