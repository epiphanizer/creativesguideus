# Suggested Instrumental Posts

These drafts are for the instrumental rollout. They hold the making-of, sonic, arrangement, and texture-driven material that did not fully belong in the more personal Instagram captions or the longform journal profiles.

## Joint Queen

Joint Queen without the vocal makes the doorway even clearer. What first hit me about John's early tracks was not an idea I could explain. It was physical: the groove, the bass movement, the open space, the little guitar scratches that felt like a whole life reordering itself in real time.

This instrumental version really lets that architecture breathe. The pocket is loose but locked. The air around the parts matters as much as the parts themselves. It is a joy track, but it is also a lesson in restraint: let the groove carry the feeling.

## Stash Daddy

The instrumental of Stash Daddy puts the recording method right out in the open. Loop the track. Hit record. Leave room for the guitar to answer without overthinking it.

What I love here is how much of the arrangement still sounds like discovery instead of design. The parts feel conversational. They are finding each other in real time. Strip away the lyric and you can hear the real thesis of the session: trust the pocket, trust the room, trust the first honest response.

## Space Cruiser

Space Cruiser might be the clearest map of the collage language that shaped Volume 1. Delays, stitched passes, drifting layers, and just enough negative space to make the whole thing feel suspended rather than crowded.

The instrumental brings the production logic to the front. You can hear how the track was assembled by feel, not by blueprint. It is less about a single heroic performance than about atmosphere accumulating until it becomes motion. A cruiser is exactly the right word for it.

## Home

Home started with a one-take guitar capture that never lost its emotional center. Even after the track kept evolving, that first performance remained the grounding element.

The instrumental makes that easier to hear. The filtered textures, the patient layering, the gradual widening of the arrangement all feel tied to that first moment of return. There is nothing flashy about it. That is part of the power. The track earns its peace by refusing to rush toward it.

## Decay

Decay in instrumental form is all about the guitar language. Not just distortion for its own sake, but a stack of human-feeling takes pushed until they stop sounding polite and start sounding necessary.

What stands out here is the way the arrangement turns wailing into structure. The heaviness is emotional before it is stylistic. You can hear the song making room for chaos without letting chaos take over the frame.

## Resolve

The instrumental version of Resolve shows how much of the song's conviction is built into pacing. The pulse is disciplined. The rise is measured. Nothing wastes motion.

Without the lyric in front, the track reads almost like a physical practice: repetition, pressure, breath, release. It carries the feeling of somebody training themselves into a new inner posture.

## Poetry

Poetry has always had a strong harmonic ache underneath the words, and the instrumental lets that ache come forward. The chord movement does a lot of the emotional work before any line is sung.

That is what makes this version interesting to me. You can hear the song's fragility and tension in the structure itself. The beauty is a little unstable. The cadence never gets too comfortable. It keeps reaching.

## Gratitude

Gratitude as an instrumental makes the closing-sequence logic of the album especially clear. The song is not trying to overwhelm the listener. It is trying to settle them.

The arrangement feels like exhale after pressure. The guitar tone, the pacing, and the way the track holds back all help it land as a true closer. It does not need to announce resolution. It sounds like it.
