BONG TOUR 
A Masala Film
Logline
A sacred bong vanishes into the Ganges and reappears on Sunset Boulevard, binding two screenwriters, an Indian-American idealist and a Tolkien superfan, to its smoke-script. They pitch “Bhang Tour”; Hollywood hears “Bong Tour,” and the bong rewrites the movie through them, until Mount Doom asks: cash it, or cast it into fire.
What it is
Bong Tour is a diaspora masala satire where Hollywood mania collides with Indian myth logic. The MacGuffin is not a ring. It is a bong, blessed and cursed by the Ganges. It plays like a cult comedy, but it lands like a fable. Fame is a drug. The industry is a trip. The only antidote is choosing what is real.
Genre and tone
Comedy, satire, adventure, with psychedelic propulsion and a grounded emotional spine.
Global comps for positioning:
The Big Lebowski (stoner philosophy)
Tropic Thunder (industry satire)
Fear and Loathing in Las Vegas (trip momentum)
The Player (meta Hollywood)
India comps for instinct
Go Goa Gone energy
Delhi Belly irreverence
Luck By Chance insider bite, filtered through a diaspora identity lens


Important note:

This is not “India as seasoning.” India is the myth engine and the emotional truth. West Bengal is origin. Kolkata is arrival. Rishikesh is reckoning. And a father embodies the Indian star system ethos: better to be a legend at home than chase mediocrity abroad.

The core cast (four, plus the predators)
Vishal
Indian American writer. Long haired. Anxious and brilliant. Caught between heritage and Hollywood validation. He believes the film means something, even when he is too high to stand upright.
Drew
Vishal’s best friend. Loud, reckless, conspiracy obsessed, and seduced by fame. Drew wants the win, even if it costs the truth.
Willie
The grounded force. She sees the machine clearly, calls out the nonsense, and ultimately becomes the real author of the outcome. She holds the bat, the line, the final cut.
Montu
A little person with scars, dignity, and mythic weight. He is not a punchline. He is the story’s spiritual gravity. Guardian of the Bong. Carrier of sacrifice. Proof that “small” is not weak.
The C lister
A washed up actor turned parasite mentor. Funny, hungry, dangerous. He ushers them into the underworld and wants his renaissance at any cost.
The A lister
A Hollywood legend. Shaman-paranoid and power drunk. He does not want a pitch. He wants an initiation.
Upper Management
Not a person. An ecosystem. The final boss is the industry itself, forever offering the sequel to keep you owned.

Story (act breakdown)
Act I: The bong enters America
West Bengal, by the Ganges.
 A maimed Montu, acid scarred and bleeding, sets a basket afloat like Moses. Inside is the Bong. It sinks, it fills, it absorbs the refuse of the sacred Ganges. Myth is literal here. Sacred and disgusting. Holy and hilarious.
Smash cut: Sunset Boulevard.
Vishal and Drew are in a van, obliterated, clutching the same bong like it is destiny. They are heading to pitch their “stoner epic” during a chaotic Hollywood moment full of strikes, volatility, and desperation.
The first act builds their rhythm: Vishal spirals, Drew charges, and neither is sober enough to realize they are walking into a larger game.
Act II: The Comedy Store initiation (and the trap springs)
Enter the C lister. He “helps” the writers, then starts shaping them. He clock punches their naivete and reframes their destiny. Even the title becomes a battleground, “Bhang Tour” versus “Bong Tour.” A diaspora cultural detail turns into an industry packaging note.
Then comes the warning, played for laughs, landing like a threat:  “The Lollipop Guild runs this town.”
 
It is absurd, until it is not.
The Comedy Store sequence escalates into a full bacchanal. Champagne in the bong. Chemical fire chaos. Backstage councils. Performers, hangers on, a sense the night itself is possessed.
Finally, the A lister arrives and refuses a normal pitch. He loads DMT into the bong and says: no words, just eyes. The trio watches the film inside their minds, joy, heartbreak, terror, until the A lister weeps and asks the moral question that becomes the spine of the movie:
 Does the little person have to die?
He greenlights it with the casual cruelty of power. Calls go out. Demands get made. Horses, permissions, favors. We see the real movie they are in, business. And now the writers are minor celebrities, pulled into negotiations, manipulation, and a widening conspiracy where everyone wants the Bong, the script, or the myth.
Act III: India, origin, sacrifice (the myth becomes a weapon)
The story bends back toward India, not as a postcard, but as source code.
We arrive in Kolkata with sensory density: smog, horns, old school Ambassadors, that humid specific chaos. Then the journey turns ominous.
The trio is pushed forward through train stations, last calls, bruised friendships. Drew’s jealousy grows as Montu and Vishal connect. The movie keeps joking, but the jokes start cutting.
Rishikesh.
 Vishal meets his father. Alive. Hidden. Complicated. A legend in his own mind and in India’s star language. He frames the theme with brutal clarity: fame is different at home. Heritage is not optional.
Flashback noir, Calcutta.
 A premiere. A chase. Violence. The “Baba Gandalfi” rule lands like scripture:

“The Bong can only preserve life. It cannot extend it.”
Climax, masala crescendo.
 Action. Betrayal. Spectacle. A mythic fight over the Bong. Catwalk, fire, obsession. A brutal fall. Montu crawling toward the river with the Bong intact.
A sharp India wink hits mid chaos: “LAGAAN…” 

Not parody, a cultural flare. Cricket logic as heroic grammar.
Montu releases the Bong into the Ganges again, completing the circle of the opening. He repeats the rule one last time: preserve, do not extend. His sacrifice anchors the comedy with real consequence.
Resolution.
A helicopter lifts off. The A lister is left behind in the dirt, power abandoned, finally made small. Willie stands there with a cricket bat, calm, watchful, unfooled.
Then the reveal of authorship: Willie produces the bound final version.
 “One script to rule them all.”
Coda.
We see the premiere of Bong Tour as a movie inside the movie. Just when it feels like closure, Upper Management reappears with the oldest drug of all: the sequel offer.

Themes (what lingers after the laughs)
Diaspora identity: you cannot outrun origin, you can only integrate it.


Fame as intoxication: the industry keeps dosing you.


Power structures: LA and Mumbai speak the same language, gatekeepers just wear different suits.


Representation with a blade: the little person is not decoration. He is the moral center. The script openly argues about how stories use bodies for catharsis.



Market and positioning
Audience
Gen Z and Millennials, stoner comedy fans, industry insider hate watchers, diaspora viewers, and festival crowds that like satire with teeth.
Platform reality
A global OTT event, English led with India chapters, with a real cult repeat watch ceiling.
Franchise seed
 The ending explicitly teases the sequel conversation with Upper Management.

Packaging notes (how we would sell it)
Attach a prestigious Indian actor as Vishal’s father, true legend energy, and treat that role like a crown.


Keep Montu dignified and iconic. Cast a real little person actor. No cheapening language in marketing.


Shoot India chapters with local creative leadership so Kolkata and Rishikesh feel lived in, not touristy.



Final thought (the sell)
Bong Tour moves like smoke. Fast, shape shifting, impossible to hold. Then it lands as something mythic: a story about who gets to tell stories, who gets used up inside them, and how the sacred keeps resurfacing even in the dirtiest places.
