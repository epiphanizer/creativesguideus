# Joint Queen — Studio Journal

**By Terry Devine**

The process of making this record started quietly, during the winter holidays. In fact, it happened so quietly, I was not at all aware of it at the time. Walls and I had been making music together for almost two years. When we started, we both brought our own songs to the project. It was a real pleasure for me to support John’s work and his songs and to get to sing my songs too. We were having a fantastic time… trying. I look back at that time and a dominant memory that has surfaced is that we weren’t at The Heart of It. Not because our songs weren’t good. Not because we couldn’t write.

Rather I think it was because the music we were playing — which we will always play — wasn’t from Us. I can only speak for myself, but I had played my tunes enough while fronting my own band that I wasn’t as nearly excited about them as I was about working with a bright, supertalented collaborator.

I see now that there was a gap that I unconsciously perceived that we both were wrestling with. We had songs. We were both born performers. We were having a fantastic time becoming friends and seeing the deep, shared themes that mattered to us as artists. We had also both poured inestimable effort and heart into prior music projects that just never developed. For one hundred reasons each.

Then John shared some recordings with me that he had been working on around New Years’. Please understand: I’m still the same twelve year-old kid who bought a record, put it on and started playing guitar with it immediately. Finding the chords, hearing the emotion. The tracks that I heard were from John Walls. They were also from my fucking Id. The sound… the style. It was the exact same moment as when I heard Public Enemy for the first time at nineteen: groove… raw emotion… power… creative synthesis. Everything changed. Everything.

As soon as I recorded one note of guitar I was into a journey that had no analogue in my life. I was working with a deeply gifted musician and recording engineer who was also becoming my best friend. And saving my life.

The situation was so very simple. But I had yet to experience pure creativity and kindness like this.

In the landscape of my mind, Joint Queen — inspired by the joy that Peace Feather brought to John’s life and to our home — was exactly where we SHOULD start… joy… grooves… an open sonic landscape.

Joy. Music is JOY for me. I believe we lose the sensation of Joy if we do not cultivate it. I had not lost that joy completely, but things were not looking good before I met John Walls.

I dreamed of such a tableau for decades. I learned to shame my dream for being so ridiculous and unachievable. For decades. “You cannot become a ‘real’ rock and roll musician… at sixty???”.

Whatever that background noise, here I was IN IT. Doing the thing, not talking about it.

So there I was, listening to this ridiculous bassline that John had recorded. I mean. This dude is good… incredible piano player, our guitar styles work phenomenally together… superexperienced engineer. But it wasn’t until John bought the legendary-to-us Mint-Green P-Bass from @freyachurros that a whole ‘nother animal showed up. I was stunned. So funky. So tight all the way through. Hearing a hundred guitar lines was automatic. Hearing all of the collage elements arrive while I was also recording some hours of original guitar was a change in my entire life.

I heard a bassline that told me I could be a part of something larger than myself in the realm that held my attention and adoration for the first time in my life. Somebody to make music with.

In the midst of the enthusiasm I was feeling at hearing a bunch of dope music being created that I was a part of, John and Peace Feather met. In addition to Love At First Sight, JW put the love energy straight into the record and Peace Feather effortlessly became his muse. As we are all naturally cut from the same bolt of cloth, our love of weed and Peace Feather’s encroyàble joint-rolling skills became the cornerstone of this joy jam.

Every Song Is Your Baby. Every lil baby is special in their own inimitable way. This tune… the groove, the energy… not only is it a perfect introduction to the joy in our hearts, it is a monument to my humble inclusion in a life that we are made for. The little guitar scratches at the beginning of the song are the sounds of my life making sense for the first time.

Here’s what joy sounds like to us. Don’t bogart it.

— Terry Devine
